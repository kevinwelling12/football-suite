## 1. Community work: Leeds United Foundation (0 to slight +)

- Registered charity no. 1137703; Charity Commission lists its reporting as up to date.
- Scale: the club's 2025/26 Foundation dedicated-fixture statement (v Arsenal) says the charity "has supported over
  55,000 people across our city, delivering more than 90,000 interventions", 90% of them children and young people.
  Other club pages give different totals (69,000 in 2023/24; "around 40,000 every year"; "43,000 last year"), so the
  size is real but the exact number is inconsistent.
- Programmes: Premier League Kicks (Hope Gatewood named the club's PL "More Than A Game" Community Captain for 2025/26,
  a former Kicks participant now a Foundation coach), education, health and employment strands.
- Budget: not verified (the CharityLens page shows placeholder figures; I did not read the filed accounts).
- No national award for the Foundation itself found for 2025-26.
- Reading: a solid, normal-sized Premier League club charity. Not at the "widely praised" level credited to Everton in
  the Community or Sunderland's Foundation of Light in the existing evidence.

## 2. Women's team (-)

- Leeds United Women play in the FA Women's National League Division One North, **tier 5** of the English pyramid, in
  2026/27. They finished third in 2025/26 (six points off the champions, no promotion) and won the West Riding County Cup.
- The club held open trials at Thorp Arch before 2026/27; the squad is not described as professional anywhere I found.
  The club frames names-and-numbers shirts as "another step in the club's ambition" to climb the pyramid.
- No published budget, professionalisation plan or WSL/WSL2 strategy found for 2025-26 or 2026-27.
- Comparison: Everton, Aston Villa, Liverpool and Brighton run funded WSL sides; Sunderland are in WSL2. Brentford's
  side is only an affiliate (and Brentford still sits at V 7). Leeds have a club-run team, which is better than none,
  but it is far from "real investment in a women's team" for a Premier League club.

## 3. Causes and inclusion (mixed: + club work, - recurring fan homophobia)

Club and fan-group work (+):
- Marching Out Together, the official LGBTQ+ supporters' group (founded 2017), works with the club on Rainbow Laces and
  Pride. Leeds won the **Professional Game Award at the Football v Homophobia Awards 2025** (Glasgow, 7 March 2025),
  first time shortlisted, ahead of Leicester and Coventry.
- Hosted a Premier League With Pride fixture v Nottingham Forest (February 2026).
- Club has campaigned for Jimmy Savile taunts aimed at Leeds fans to be classed as tragedy chanting (and says it
  "disapproves of retaliatory chants from our own supporters").
- Refugees: Foundation took part in Amnesty's Football Welcomes (2021; older, nothing newer found). Mental health is
  part of the Kicks programme; no separate club-level stance found.
- Racism: in February 2025 Ethan Ampadu and Largie Ramazani were racially abused by a visiting Sunderland supporter at
  Elland Road; the club supported the players and the police case (arrest, later a three-year banning order). Leeds
  were the victims here, not the source.

Fan incidents (-):
- **April 2026 (current):** FA Cup semi-final v Chelsea at Wembley (26 April 2026). The Metropolitan Police reported
  16 Leeds fans for consideration of prosecution for homophobic chanting ("rent boy" around Wembley Way and Wembley
  Park). Marching Out Together condemned it. I found **no club statement or club bans** for this incident (unverified
  either way).
- **March 2023:** homophobic chanting v Brighton at Elland Road. FA fined Leeds **GBP 150,000**; the commission noted
  "surprise and disappointment at the lack of evidence as to the action taken by Leeds United against any supporters".
  One fan later got a five-year banning order and a club ban.
- **November/December 2021:** "Chelsea rent boy" chants at Conor Gallagher during a Rainbow Laces match; club condemned.
- The same behaviour continues (2021, 2023, 2026), so under the Round 5 rule it does not fade to half.
- Historic Service Crew hooligan element: older than 5 years, not current (neutral, as in existing evidence).

## 5. Ticket pricing and treatment of fans (+, with a caveat)

- Cheapest adult season ticket: **GBP 495 (2025/26) -> GBP 510 (2026/27), +3%**. Among the cheapest in the Premier
  League (Planet Football, 16 April 2026): Man City 425, Leeds 510, Brentford 520, Sunderland 550, Forest 575,
  Brighton 630, Liverpool 734.50, Everton 770, Spurs 856, Arsenal 1,171.
- 2026/27: a single pricing structure (same price for the same section for the first time); Category 2/3 holders rise
  with inflation; Category 1 rises vary by stand and age; concession age bands unchanged.
- Membership (needed for ballots and priority): prices **frozen** for 2026/27 (My Leeds GBP 50 adult / 30 junior; My
  Leeds+ 75 / 45; GBP 5 less on auto-renew). The Supporter Advisory Board says it helped secure the freeze. Average
  2025/26 home ballot success was 50%.
- Away tickets: the Premier League's GBP 30 away cap applies (league-wide, not a Leeds credit).
- Friction: in March 2025 the Supporters' Trust said the club took its feedback on 2025/26 prices (freeze, reverse the
  2024 concession changes, review matchday prices) "but haven't acted on any of it". Season-ticket waiting lists are
  long and the SAB chair flags fairness and transparency concerns. I could not verify the "legacy fans" row in the
  existing evidence beyond this.
- No fan protest with a forced reversal (as at Liverpool) found.

The pluses (cheap tickets, the FvH award, a decent foundation, a Category One academy) roughly offset the
minuses (a tier-5 women's team, a new betting partner, recurring homophobic chanting). The 2026 Wembley chanting and
the betting partner are new since the 6.0 was set, and the women's-team gap is larger than "typical": that tips it
slightly below a typical 6.

Comparisons (V from scores.py / decimal_bands.json):
- **Sunderland 5.8**: betting sleeve and betting chest (heavier sponsor minus than Leeds), but a WSL2 women's team, the
  larger Foundation of Light and a stronger academy; similar cheap tickets. Leeds lighter on sponsors, weaker on women's
  team and academy: about level, a touch below.
- **Fulham 5.8**: neutral sponsors, high top prices, no funded women's team. Leeds have far cheaper tickets but a
  betting partner and the chanting record: about level.
- **Crystal Palace 6.0**: betting sleeve, Palace for Life, productive academy, fan campaigning. Palace's academy output
  is clearly better than Leeds'; Leeds a little below.
- **Brentford 7.0**: board seat for the Trust, low prices, no betting; Leeds lack the fan power and add betting.
- **Everton 7.0**: Everton in the Community and a WSL side outweigh their Stake sleeve; Leeds have neither strength.
- **Liverpool 8.2**: fan-forced price reversal, Hillsborough campaign, WSL side, foundation; not comparable.

If Kevin treats around-the-ground betting signage as no effect (only shirt/sleeve/naming), the case is 5.8-5.9; if the
2026 chanting draws a club sanction or a fresh FA charge, 5.5 would be defensible. Staying at 6.0 is also defensible
if the cheap tickets are weighted more; I would not go above 6.0 on this evidence.
